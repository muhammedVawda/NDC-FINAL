How to Deal With Multiple Creditors at Once

Owing several different creditors makes everything feel more chaotic than it needs to. Here's how to bring some order to it before deciding what to do next.

Published 8 September 2026 · 2 min read

When debt is spread across several credit providers — a credit card here, a store account there, a personal loan somewhere else — it's easy to lose track of the overall picture. That's usually the first problem worth solving, before deciding on a strategy.

1. Build one single list

Every account, its outstanding balance, its minimum monthly repayment, and who it's with. Scattered across statements and apps, this information is hard to act on; in one place, patterns become obvious — which accounts are largest, which are most urgent, which are manageable.

2. Understand the difference between restructuring and consolidating

Formal debt review restructures your existing repayments into a single, revised monthly plan — it is not a new loan or a consolidation loan product. That distinction matters: you're not taking on new debt to pay off old debt, you're changing the terms of what you already owe.

3. Know that creditors don't have to agree to everything

Whether you're pursuing formal debt review or informal debt mediation, a credit provider isn't automatically obliged to accept a proposed plan. Under debt review, unresolved disputes have a route through the courts or the National Consumer Tribunal; under debt mediation, a creditor can simply decline.

4. A single combined plan doesn't remove complexity overnight

Even once a restructured plan is in place, it still needs to be maintained — consistent payments, kept up over time. It simplifies the structure of what you owe, but doesn't remove the need for an ongoing, realistic budget alongside it.

Frequently asked

Is a combined repayment plan the same as a consolidation loan?

No. Restructuring your repayments through debt review is not a new loan — it's a revised repayment plan across your existing credit agreements.
