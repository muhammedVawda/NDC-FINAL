Common Debt Mistakes to Avoid

Some of the most common ways a manageable debt situation becomes a harder one — and how to steer around them.

Published 15 September 2026 · 2 min read

Avoiding the problem instead of assessing it

It's a natural response to avoid looking closely at a stressful number, but avoidance tends to narrow your options over time rather than protect you. An honest, early assessment usually leaves more paths open than waiting does.

Trusting a guarantee that sounds too clean

Be cautious of anyone promising a specific reduced interest rate, a fast debt review removal, or a guaranteed outcome before actually assessing your situation. The National Credit Regulator has specifically warned about businesses charging large upfront fees for promised fast removal from debt review — and under the National Credit Act, that kind of service generally can't be paid for upfront before it's actually delivered.

Confusing debt review with a new loan

Debt review restructures your existing repayments — it isn't a consolidation loan, and you're not borrowing new money to solve the problem. Going into it expecting a loan product can lead to real confusion about what's actually happening to your finances.

Assuming debt mediation gives the same protection as debt review

They're genuinely different: formal debt review carries legal protection from further action while you comply, with an escalation route through the courts or the National Consumer Tribunal if a creditor won't agree. Debt mediation doesn't carry that statutory protection — a creditor can decline a proposal or still pursue legal action during the negotiation.

Not revisiting your budget once a plan is in place

A restructured repayment plan only works alongside a realistic budget that's kept up to date. Income and expenses change — treating the plan as “set and forget” is one of the more common ways people fall behind again.

Frequently asked

If I've made one of these mistakes already, is it too late?

No — these are common, and a consultant can help you understand your current situation and realistic next steps, regardless of what's already happened.
