Debt Counselling

How Debt Counselling Works in South Africa

A plain-language walkthrough of what actually happens during formal debt review — from your first assessment to what protection it gives you along the way.

Published 4 August 2026 · Updated 15 September 2026 · 2 min read

“Debt counselling” and “debt review” refer to the same formal process in South Africa — a legally regulated route, introduced under the National Credit Act (NCA) and overseen by the National Credit Regulator (NCR), for consumers who are over-indebted. It's worth understanding the shape of the process before deciding whether it's right for you.

Step 1: Assessment

A registered debt counsellor reviews your income, monthly expenses, and everything you owe. This determines whether you're genuinely over-indebted — meaning your current repayments, alongside reasonable living costs, aren't manageable. Not everyone who applies qualifies; it depends on this assessment, not just on wanting the protection debt review offers.

Step 2: A restructured repayment plan

If you qualify, the debt counsellor works out a single, consolidated monthly repayment plan across your credit agreements, sized to what you can actually afford. This plan then needs to be proposed to your credit providers.

Step 3: Credit provider agreement (or a court/Tribunal route)

Credit providers aren't automatically obliged to accept a proposed plan. Where an agreement can't be reached, formal debt review has a legal escalation route — through the courts or the National Consumer Tribunal — that debt mediation, the informal alternative, does not have.

What protection does it actually give you?

While you're under formal debt review and complying with your plan, credit providers are restricted from taking further legal action against you for the debts included in it. That protection is specific to the formal process — it doesn't apply to informal negotiation (debt mediation), and it's why the two are genuinely different products, not two names for the same thing.

What it isn't

Debt review isn't a new loan or a debt consolidation loan — you're not borrowing more money. It's a restructuring of what you already owe. It also isn't guaranteed to lower your interest rate or monthly payment by a specific amount; what changes depends entirely on what's agreed with each individual credit provider.

Your credit record during the process

Being under formal debt review is reflected on your credit record for as long as you're in the process. This is a normal, expected part of how the system works — not a penalty — and it's removed once you exit through a legally recognised route, most commonly by receiving a clearance certificate after your included debts are paid in full.

Frequently asked

Does debt counselling guarantee my interest rates will drop?

No. Any change to your rates or payments depends on what's agreed with each credit provider individually — it isn't a fixed or guaranteed outcome.

Can I be forced into debt review?

No. It follows an assessment and your own application — it isn't something that can be applied to you without your involvement.
