How Do I Know If I Am Over-Indebted?

Over-indebtedness has a specific legal meaning in South Africa — and some practical warning signs that usually show up well before it does.

Published 21 September 2026 · 2 min read

“Over-indebted” isn't just a description of feeling stretched. Under the National Credit Act it's a specific finding — and it covers more than being unable to pay today.

The legal meaning

Section 79 of the National Credit Act frames over-indebtedness in terms of whether a consumer is or will be unable to satisfy their obligations under all their credit agreements in a timely manner. Importantly, it extends beyond an existing inability to a future or imminent one — you do not have to already be in default.

A debt counsellor makes a determination on this after assessing your income, expenses and debts. A court then declares over-indebtedness and grants relief.

Practical warning signs

  • You use credit to cover ordinary monthly expenses like food, fuel or electricity.
  • You take new credit largely to service existing credit.
  • You pay minimums on revolving accounts and the balance never really falls.
  • You're juggling which account to skip each month.
  • Essential expenses plus debt repayments exceed take-home pay, so any shortfall is financed.

None of these individually proves anything. A pattern of them, sustained over months, is usually a structural problem rather than a run of bad months.

A rough self-check

Add your take-home pay. Subtract essential living costs. Subtract total monthly debt repayments. If the result is consistently negative or near zero with no buffer, you're relying on something to go right every month — which is precisely what fails when a car needs repairs or a medical cost appears.

The budget calculator on this site does this arithmetic for you and gives you a summary. It's a general budget picture, not a formal assessment.

Why acting early matters

Because over-indebtedness under the Act includes a future inability to pay, you don't have to wait until accounts are in arrears to seek help. Earlier generally means more options remain open — once matters reach judgment stage, the range of realistic outcomes narrows.

Frequently asked

Do I have to be behind on payments to be over-indebted?

No. Section 79 of the NCA covers a future or imminent inability to meet obligations, not only an existing one.

Can I decide myself that I'm over-indebted?

You can recognise the signs, but the finding follows an assessment by a debt counsellor and a declaration by a court.

Does the calculator tell me if I qualify for debt review?

No. It gives a general budget summary. Qualifying requires a full assessment of your circumstances.
