Understanding Your Credit Profile

Your credit profile affects more than loan approvals. Here's what it actually reflects, and what does (and doesn't) show up on it during debt review.

Published 25 August 2026 · 2 min read

Your credit profile is a record, held by registered credit bureaus, of how you've managed credit agreements over time — accounts opened, repayment history, and any adverse listings. It's used by credit providers to assess new applications, but it also matters simply as a picture of your own financial history.

What actually affects it

Missed or late payments, the number and type of accounts you hold, how long you've held credit, and formal processes like debt review or judgments are all reflected on your profile over time.

Debt review and your credit record

Being under formal debt review is reflected on your credit record for the duration of the process. This is a normal, expected part of the system — not a punishment — and it's removed once you exit through a legally recognised route, such as receiving a clearance certificate after your debts are paid off in full.

A word of caution

The National Credit Regulator has publicly warned consumers about businesses promising fast debt review removal for a large upfront fee. Under section 126A(3) of the National Credit Act, a business offering to help remove information from your credit record generally can't take payment from you before that service has actually been carried out. If something sounds like an unusually fast, guaranteed fix, it's worth being cautious.

Rebuilding after debt review

Once you exit debt review properly — with a clearance certificate, where applicable — your credit record reflects that. Rebuilding a strong credit profile from there is a gradual process based on consistent, responsible use of credit going forward; there's no shortcut that skips this.

Frequently asked

Can my income improving alone get me removed from debt review?

No. Removal follows specific legal routes and requires an assessment — an improved income alone doesn't automatically qualify you.

What is a clearance certificate?

It's the document a registered debt counsellor issues once the debts covered by your restructured plan have been paid off in full, confirming you've completed debt review, used to update your status with the credit bureaus.

Want to talk through your own situation?

This article is general information, not a personal assessment. A consultant can look at your actual numbers and explain what applies to you.
