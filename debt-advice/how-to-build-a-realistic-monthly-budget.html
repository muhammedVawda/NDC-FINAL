How to Build a Realistic Monthly Budget

A budget only works if it reflects what actually happens in your bank account. Here's a straightforward way to build one you can stick to.

Published 18 August 2026 · 2 min read

Most budgets fail for a boring reason: they're built around what someone thinks their spending should look like, not what it actually is. A realistic budget starts with real numbers.

1. Start with take-home pay, not gross salary

Use what actually lands in your account after tax and deductions — that's the number you can genuinely work with, not your salary on paper.

2. List fixed essentials first

Rent or bond, transport to work, groceries, utilities, insurance, and minimum debt repayments. These are the costs that don't flex much month to month — get an honest total before anything else.

3. Track variable spending for one real month

Eating out, subscriptions, incidental purchases — these are usually where a budget quietly falls apart. Look at an actual bank statement rather than estimating from memory; most people underestimate this category significantly.

4. Compare income to total outgoings honestly

If essentials plus debt repayments already exceed your income, that's important information — not a failure on your part, but a sign that your current repayment structure may not be sustainable, and worth an honest conversation with a consultant rather than a stricter budget alone.

5. Revisit it monthly, not once

A budget is a working document. Income and expenses shift — a new account, a rent increase, a change in hours. Treat it as something you check in on regularly, not a one-time exercise.
