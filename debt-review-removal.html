Debt Review Removal

For consumers already under formal debt review who want to understand their options for exiting the process.

What it is

Debt review removal refers to the legally recognised ways a consumer can exit the formal debt review process. It is not a single button that gets pressed. It follows specific routes set out in the National Credit Act, and it requires an assessment of your situation by a registered debt counsellor, and in some cases a court or the National Consumer Tribunal.

Two different situations, two different routes

This applies only to consumers who are currently under formal debt review. Which route is relevant depends on where you are in the process:

If your restructured debts have been paid off in full: your debt counsellor confirms that the accounts covered by your plan have genuinely been settled according to its terms, then issues a clearance certificate. That certificate is used to update your status with the credit bureaus. A court order isn’t usually needed for this route.

If you want to exit before your restructured debts are fully paid off: once a debt counsellor has recommended that you’re over-indebted, you generally can’t simply opt out. Exiting at that stage normally means applying to a court (or, in some cases, the National Consumer Tribunal) to reconsider your debt review status, often called a withdrawal application. This takes time, requires evidence that your circumstances have genuinely changed, and isn’t guaranteed to succeed.

What the assessment involves

We review your current debt review status, what’s been paid off against your restructured plan, and your present income and expenses. This tells us which of the two routes above, if either, realistically applies to you, and what evidence would be needed to support it.

What the next step looks like

Depending on your situation, the next step may be applying for a clearance certificate once your restructured debts are settled, or preparing a court application to have your debt review order reconsidered. Your debt counsellor is responsible for notifying the National Credit Regulator and updating the credit bureaus once a legally recognised exit is confirmed. This is how your debt review flag is formally cleared.

A warning about “debt review removal” scams

The National Credit Regulator has publicly warned consumers about businesses that promise fast debt review removal in exchange for a large upfront fee. Under section 126A(3) of the National Credit Act, a business offering to help remove or amend information held about you at a credit bureau generally may not take payment from you until that service has actually been carried out.

Be cautious of anyone who guarantees removal, asks you to stop paying your creditors, or wants full payment upfront before any work has been done.

Important limitations

  • Removal is not automatic just because your income has improved. It requires a proper assessment and, usually, formal evidence.
  • Only a registered debt counsellor can issue a clearance certificate, and only once the debts covered by your plan have genuinely been settled according to its terms.
  • Exiting before your debts are settled generally requires a court or the National Consumer Tribunal to be involved, which takes time and isn’t guaranteed to succeed.
  • Your credit record reflects your debt review history until a legally recognised exit is processed and reported. It isn’t erased instantly.
  • Every case is different. The specific route that applies to you depends on your paperwork, your creditors and how far along your debt review process is.
